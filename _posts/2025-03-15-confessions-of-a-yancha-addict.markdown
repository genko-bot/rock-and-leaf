**Dear Reader,**  

I must confess something. It humbles me to admit it, but there’s no use in living in hiding—I am a yancha addict.  

It started innocently enough: a sampler pack from **Old Ways Tea** and a dedicated yancha pot, both acquired over a year ago. I loved the fusion of floral and toasty, chocolate and mineral. Yancha satisfied a craving I didn’t even realize I had for good tea.  

But like a flash in the pan, my months-long love affair faded into a distant memory. I drank tea occasionally after that, until a recent impulse reminded me just how much I enjoyed it.  

Having spent the first months of 2025 focused on finances and the implications of a real job, getting back into tea was a terrible decision from a financial perspective. I’m easily swayed by random Redditors, turning simple sample orders into never-ending wish lists, often forgetting why I added certain teas to my cart in the first place. Handmade teaware, with its ability to elevate the tea-drinking experience, only deepens my obsession.  

That said, I don’t regret my purchases. Even impulse buys have taught me more about what I like and dislike. Take, for example, my recent sample order from **Yunnan Sourcing**. While its primary purpose was to explore a range of sheng puerh, I couldn’t resist slipping in some yancha. I picked up 50g of *Buddha's Hand* and 50g of *Ai Jiao.*  

Ultimately, I’ve been disappointed by both. I’ve drunk copious amounts of *Buddha's Hand* over the past few weeks, and each time, I find myself thinking: *Was that it?* My experience with the *Ai Jiao* was similar. 

I’ve experimented with different waters—tap, bottled spring from various sources, even filtered—to no avail. Adjusting parameters like leaf-to-water ratio or temperature has made little impact. Frankly, neither tea tastes like much of anything at all.  

Yancha seems to be one of those teas where cheap almost always means compromised quality.  

When I try teas, I make an effort to forget what I’ve read about them and experience them as they are. Of course, this is ultimately impossible—labels like *Old Tree* or *Handmade* undoubtedly influence perception.  

This experience begs the question: *What makes good tea good?* Why did Yunnan Sourcing’s yancha disappoint while Old Ways Tea’s enchants?  

To investigate further, my yancha-starved brain rationalized yet another purchase—from **Old Ways Tea.**
